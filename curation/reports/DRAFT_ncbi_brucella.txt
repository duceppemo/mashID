Dear GenBank/RefSeq staff,

This follows our reports of 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23 on Brucella, Salmonella and Shigella
assemblies. Rebuilding our mashID Brucella reference database with those corrections applied
surfaced ten further assemblies whose organism name is not supported by their sequence. As before we
are reporting them for review with the submitters and are not requesting a taxonomy change.

Why these ten and not others: the classical Brucella species are a single genomospecies, 0.2 to
0.5% apart, so two assemblies carrying different species names and sitting 0.00004 to 0.0002 apart
(Mash, k=21, s=10000) cannot both be correctly named. Each such pair in our database was then put to
two independent tests that do not depend on distance:

  - the species-specific multiplex real-time PCR of Hoover E., Chmara J., Duceppe M., Charron P.,
    Lloyd D., Huang H., Surujballi O., Thanthrige-Don N., Arnold K., Hazelwood J. and Kang M.
    (2026), Microbiol Spectr 14:e02974-25, scored in silico: an assay counts as held only when all
    three of its oligos occur full length and mismatch free, the primers face each other, the probe
    lies between them and the amplicon is the published length;
  - MLST-9 (PubMLST brucella scheme, mlst 2.33.1) run over all 460 genomes of our dereplicated
    reference set, so that an ST is read against the distribution of deposited names that carry it
    rather than against a handful of controls.

In every case below the two agree, and where an ST is informative the dissenting name is the
assembly the assay contradicts.


1. FOUR ASSEMBLIES DEPOSITED AS B. ABORTUS THAT TYPE AS B. MELITENSIS

NCBI's ANI check already flags all four as mismatch, so these may be known to you.

    accession        deposited          MLST-9   assay held      our reading
    GCA_041861985.1  Brucella abortus   ST8      B. melitensis   B. melitensis
    GCA_016091955.1  Brucella abortus   ST8      B. melitensis   B. melitensis
    GCA_016091965.1  Brucella abortus   ST8      B. melitensis   B. melitensis
    GCA_018604785.1  Brucella abortus   ST8      B. melitensis   B. melitensis

Across our 460 typed references ST8 is carried by 10 deposited B. melitensis and 1 deposited
B. abortus, that one being GCA_041861985.1. GCA_041861985.1 shares all nine MLST alleles with
GCA_003516085.1, a deposited B. melitensis 0.000096 away, and the B. abortus assay is absent from
all four assemblies.


2. THREE ASSEMBLIES WHOSE DEPOSITED NAME IS CONTRADICTED BY BOTH TESTS

    GCA_000250775.2  "Brucella melitensis S66"   ->  B. suis
        MLST-9 ST14 with all nine alleles identical to the deposited B. suis GCA_055657665.1,
        GCA_000331635.1 and GCA_000292105.2. ST14 across our references is suis 5 to melitensis 1,
        the melitensis being this assembly. The B. suis assay is held in full, the B. melitensis
        assay absent. Mash 0.000041 to GCA_055657665.1 against 0.000303 to its nearest
        B. melitensis. A fourth, independent line: in held-out validation of our previous database,
        all seven B. suis genomes that were misidentified as B. melitensis matched this one
        reference, and correcting it raised species concordance from 93.0% to 96.8%.

    GCA_000250835.2  "Brucella melitensis 16M13W" ->  B. abortus
        MLST-9 ST1, eight of nine alleles shared with the deposited B. abortus GCA_000742195.1 and
        GCA_000163115.1; ST1 is abortus 9 to melitensis 1, the melitensis being this assembly. The
        B. abortus assay is held in full, the B. melitensis assay absent.

    GCA_000292125.1  "Brucella suis S2-30"        ->  B. melitensis
        MLST-9 ST7, eight of nine alleles shared with the deposited B. melitensis GCA_002191935.1;
        ST7 is melitensis 2 to suis 1, the suis being this assembly. The B. melitensis assay is held
        in full, the B. suis assay absent. match_status for this record is synonym_match.


3. TWO ASSEMBLIES DEPOSITED AS B. MELITENSIS THAT ARE NOT B. MELITENSIS

    GCA_055389085.1  "Brucella melitensis"  ->  B. canis
        None of the three species assays is held. That is how all five of our deposited B. canis
        references behave and unlike all six B. melitensis controls, and this is a two-contig
        assembly, so absence is not an artefact of fragmentation. MLST-9 ST20, shared with the
        deposited B. canis GCA_000530495.1 0.000060 away, against 0.00118 to its nearest
        B. melitensis.

    GCA_009823695.1  "Brucella melitensis"  ->  B. suis
        The B. suis assay is held in full and the B. melitensis assay is absent; Mash 0.000117 to
        GCA_014884525.1, a deposited B. suis biovar 3. We cannot exclude B. canis, which is a
        lineage within the B. suis group, so B. suis is the more general of the two readings. ST17
        is uninformative: only two of our references carry it.


4. ONE ASSEMBLY DEPOSITED AS B. INOPINATA THAT IS A CLASSICAL BRUCELLA

    GCA_000662015.2  "Brucella inopinata BO1"  ->  B. canis

        This assembly sits 0.0234, 0.0237 and 0.0243 from the three deposited B. inopinata
        assemblies GCA_030270765.1, GCA_000182725.1 and GCA_900095155.1 - as far as those sit from
        the classical species - while sitting 0.0011 from B. suis and 0.0012 from B. canis, that is,
        inside the classical cluster. GCA_000182725.1 carries the same strain designation BO1 and is
        0.0237 away, so the two assemblies deposited under that strain name are not the same
        organism, and we think that is the more useful observation for triage than our own species
        call.

        The species call B. canis is from Table S5 of the paper cited above, which reaches it by
        Mash, SNP, PubMLST and phylogenetic analysis. Our own evidence establishes only that this is
        a classical member of the suis-canis group and not B. inopinata: it holds the B. suis assay,
        and the regional presence/absence profile we used elsewhere matches a deposited B. suis, so
        we could not separate suis from canis independently.

        This record carries match_status = species_match, at 97.89% ANI to the B. inopinata type.
        We mention it because the pass is technically correct and still misleading: every Brucella
        sits within roughly 97% ANI of every other, so a declared species clears the 95% threshold
        without being the closest. For this genus the field cannot distinguish a correct name from a
        wrong one, and five of the ten records above carry species_match or synonym_match.
