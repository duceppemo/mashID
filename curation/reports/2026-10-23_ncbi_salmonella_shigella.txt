Dear GenBank/RefSeq staff,

This follows our report of 2026-10-22 concerning 3 Brucella and 15 Salmonella enterica
assemblies. Rebuilding our mashID reference databases with those corrections applied surfaced 15
further assemblies whose organism name is not supported by their sequence. As before we are
reporting them for review with the submitters and are not requesting a taxonomy change.

The previous report listed records one at a time. This one is organised by submission, because
that turned out to be the unit in which the errors occur, and we think that makes them easier for
you to triage. Methods are unchanged: SISTR 1.0.2 cgMLST for Salmonella subspecies, mlst 2.33.1
with the PubMLST ecoli_achtman_4 scheme for Shigella, controls drawn from the same assembly set.

3. SALMONELLA ENTERICA, Nanjing Agricultural University, released 2021-02-17

This one runs the other way: a subsp. enterica name on a genome that is not subsp. enterica. We
mention the direction because it means the record would not be caught by screening the rare
subspecies names, which is how we found all the others.

  GCF_016903075.1  "Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica"
                   ->  Salmonella enterica subsp. salamae

      SISTR cgMLST assigns subsp. salamae, serovar II 58:d:z8, on 330 matching alleles, the
      maximum the scheme reports. It lies 0.000542 (mash, k=21, s=10000) from GCF_002034565.1,
      which is deposited as "subsp. salamae serovar 58:d:z6" and which SISTR independently
      confirms as salamae II 58:d:z6, also on 330 alleles. Same serogroup, adjacent serovar
      designation: two subsp. salamae genomes of which only one is named consistently.
      match_status = derived_species_match, i.e. ANI confirmed the species and not the subspecies.


4. SHIGELLA, submitter "JCVI", released 2017-09-12

  GCF_002290505.1  "Shigella flexneri"  ->  Shigella sonnei

      ST58 in the Achtman E. coli scheme, with all seven alleles identical to GCF_002248345.1
      (adk-6 fumC-4 gyrB-4 icd-16 mdh-24 purA-8 recA-14), which the same submitter deposited as
      S. sonnei on 2017-08-11. ST58 is therefore positively attested among your S. sonnei records
      rather than merely absent from S. flexneri. Six of the seven also match the S. sonnei
      assembly GCF_013374815.1 (ST155), differing only at gyrB.
      All five S. flexneri controls we typed are ST245 and share only adk-6 with it.
      Mash distance 0.000036 to GCF_002290495.1 - the consecutive accession in the same
      submission, which we reported to you separately as S. flexneri that is really S. sonnei -
      against 0.005470 to its nearest S. flexneri, a 150-fold contrast.
      Note GCF_002290495.1 and GCF_002290505.1 cannot both be correctly named.

  We typed the other ten members of that submission and they need nothing: they spread over nine
  unrelated sequence types. Two of them (GCF_002317065.1 "S. sonnei" ST10, GCF_002317935.1
  "S. boydii" ST48) carry STs more typical of E. coli than of their deposited species, and
  GCF_002290485.1 ("S. boydii") and GCF_002317875.1 ("S. sonnei") share ST877 and all seven
  alleles, but they sit 0.003349 apart with each closer to an E. coli assembly than to the other,
  which we read as ordinary Shigella/E. coli polyphyly. We are not making a claim about any of
  those four.


5. ONE OBSERVATION THAT MAY BE USEFUL

None of the 27 Salmonella assemblies in section 1 and 2 carries match_status = subspecies_match;
all are species_match or derived_species_match. Combined with the previous report, where
subspecies_match never disagreed with SISTR in 13 conclusive records, the field continues to look
like a sound positive signal and its absence like a reliable marker of "not yet verified at
subspecies level". A single submission being able to carry twelve copies of one wrong subspecies
name is consistent with that: nothing upstream was in a position to contradict it.
